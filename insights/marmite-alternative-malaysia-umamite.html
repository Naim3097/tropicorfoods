Looking for Marmite in Malaysia? Meet UMAMITE.

Marmite has quietly left Malaysian shelves. Here is what happened, and the halal-certified yeast-extract spread made in Malaysia that fills the gap.

Insights Updated August 2026 By Tropicor Foods

If you have been staring at an empty spot on the supermarket shelf where Marmite used to sit, you are not imagining it. And you are definitely not alone.

What happened to Marmite in Malaysia?

Unilever Asia has confirmed that Marmite was delisted from Malaysia and Singapore effective 1 April 2026. The company pointed to long-running production problems at its sourcing unit, and the news was widely reported by Mothership and Business Today in July. In the UK, the brand itself is reportedly under review.

What that means in practice: whatever is left on shelves is old stock, and online resellers have pushed prices to several times what a jar used to cost. Some listings in Singapore have crossed the S$50 mark. For families who grew up on Marmite toast, that stings. For the many Malaysian restaurants with Marmite chicken (妈蜜鸡) on the menu, it is a supply problem with no end date.

The good news: you do not have to import it

There is a local answer. UMAMITE is a yeast-extract spread made by FEEGOH, the seasoning brand of Tropicor Foods. We have been manufacturing food ingredients in Malaysia for over 30 years, and we make UMAMITE right here, in our own halal-certified facilities.

What is UMAMITE?

UMAMITE is a rich, dark spread in the same family as Marmite: a true yeast extract, not a soy sauce workaround. The flavour is deep, savoury and concentrated, the kind of umami you normally only get from hours of reduction.

Two things matter for Malaysian buyers in particular. First, it is halal-certified and produced under JAKIM, GMP and MESTI standards. That is worth checking before you reach for imported substitutes, because some of them, Bovril included, are beef based. Second, it is made locally. No shipping lottery, no reseller mark-ups, no wondering whether the next jar will ever arrive.

You can read the full product details on the UMAMITE product page.

How people actually use it

On toast, the way it has always been done: a thin scrape over hot butter. In the kitchen it goes much further. A spoonful builds the base for Marmite chicken and other dai chao dishes, deepens stocks, soups and gravies, and works into marinades and glazes for wings and ribs. Food manufacturers use yeast extract the same way at production scale, as a consistent umami ingredient in snacks, sauces and ready meals. If you want the longer explanation of how yeast extract works, we cover it in our guide to yeast extract and umami.

It is the same category of product, a concentrated yeast-extract spread, made to our own recipe. It plays the same role on toast and in cooking, and it is produced locally under halal certification. Full details are on the UMAMITE product page.
