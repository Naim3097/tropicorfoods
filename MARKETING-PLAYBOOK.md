# Tropicor Foods — Marketing & Visibility Playbook
### Google Business Profile setup + ongoing actions for the marketing team

No technical skills needed for anything in this document. Written for whoever
handles marketing day to day.

---

## Part 1 — Google Business Profile (one-time setup)

The Business Profile is what appears in Google Maps and in the box on the right
of search results. For a manufacturer in Klang it is how buyers searching
"food manufacturer Klang" or "halal seasoning supplier Selangor" find you.

### 1. Business name — exactly as registered

Use the real trading name only. Do **not** add keywords like
"Tropicor Foods - Halal Seasoning Manufacturer". Google penalises keyword
stuffing in the name field and profiles get suspended for it. The name must
match the website and your other listings character for character.

### 2. Categories — the single biggest ranking factor

- **Primary:** Food manufacturer
- **Secondary:** add what Google offers from these — Food products supplier,
  Wholesale food store, Manufacturer, Spice store

The primary category matters more than everything else on this list combined.
If you get one thing right, get this right.

### 3. Keep the address public

Do not set the profile as "service area only" and hide the address. You have a
real factory that customers and auditors visit. A verified physical address can
appear in local map results; a hidden one usually cannot.

Add service areas as well: Klang, Shah Alam, Petaling Jaya, Kuala Lumpur,
Selangor, and Malaysia generally.

### 4. Hours — including public holidays

Set real office hours. Then use "Special hours" for Malaysian public holidays
(Hari Raya, CNY, Deepavali, etc). Profiles that show as open when they are
closed collect bad reviews; profiles with no hours look abandoned.

### 5. Description (750 characters)

Write naturally, but make sure these appear: halal certified (JAKIM), GMP,
MESTI, seasonings, premixes, fruit purees, food ingredients, OEM, private
label, foodservice, over 30 years, 25 countries.

### 6. Website link — use this exact URL

```
https://www.tropicorfoods.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp
```

The tag on the end is what lets Analytics tell you how many enquiries came from
the Business Profile. Without it, those visits blend into general Google traffic
and the profile's value is invisible.

### 7. Photos — the profile lives or dies on these

Upload at least 10 to start, then a few every month:

- Building exterior (helps Google match you to Street View)
- Production floor and packing lines
- Certification marks (halal, GMP, MESTI, HACCP)
- Product range shots
- Team at work, events, trade shows

Profiles with regular photo activity consistently outperform static ones.

### 8. Products and services

Add each range as its own entry, price "on request", each linking to the
matching page on the website:

Seasonings & Pre-Mix · Fruit Products · Food Ingredients · Customisation & OEM ·
FEEGOH UMAMITE

Then services: OEM manufacturing, private label, custom formulation, halal
certification support, contract packing.

### 9. Q&A — seed it yourself

You are allowed to post questions and answer them. Use the ones you actually get
by email:

- Are your products halal certified?
- What is the minimum order quantity?
- Do you manufacture under our own brand?
- Can we request samples?
- Do you export?

### 10. Turn on messaging only if someone watches it

Google shows response rate publicly. An unanswered message queue looks worse
than no messaging at all.

## Part 2 — Ongoing actions for the marketing team

Ordered by impact. None of these need a developer.

### Reviews (highest impact, hardest to do)

B2B review counts are usually low, so even 5 to 10 genuine ones put you ahead of
most competitors locally.

- Ask long-standing distributors, restaurant clients and foodservice buyers.
  The best moment is right after a smooth delivery or a successful audit.
- Get the short review link from the Business Profile dashboard and put it in
  email signatures and post-delivery follow-ups.
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad. Replies are public and show the
  business is active. A calm reply to a complaint reads better than no reply.
- **Never** post fake reviews or reviews written by staff. The testimonials
  currently on the website are placeholders supplied for design purposes and
  must not be posted to Google. Fake reviews get profiles suspended, and
  recovering a suspended profile is far harder than earning real reviews.

### Business directories and listings

Every accurate listing of your name, address and phone strengthens the profile.
Keep the details **identical** everywhere — same spelling, same phone format.

Malaysian and trade directories worth doing:

- MATRADE exporter directory
- FMM (Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers) directory
- Halal Development Corporation / halal supplier directories
- Yellow Pages Malaysia
- Alibaba, Global Sources, Kompass, Europages for export enquiries

### Social media

- Website link in **every** bio: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn
- LinkedIn matters most here, because buyers are businesses
- Share each Insights article when published
- Post factory, certification and product content — it doubles as GBP photo
  material
- Keep the business name, address and phone identical to the website

### Put the website everywhere offline

Free and consistently underused:

- Email signatures for the whole team
- Quotations, invoices, catalogues, spec sheets
- WhatsApp Business profile
- Product packaging and cartons
- Trade show banners and name cards
- Delivery vehicles

### Content the team can create without a writer

- **Answer real questions.** Every question a customer emails is a potential
  page or post. If one buyer asked, others are searching it.
- **Customer stories** with permission — even a short paragraph on how a
  restaurant uses a product.
- **Photos of new products** the moment they exist.
- **Trade shows and events** — attending usually earns a listing on the event
  website, which is a genuine link back to you.

### Links from other websites

Links are what push a site up over time. Realistic sources:

- Trade association membership pages
- Supplier or partner pages on customers' websites, ask for it
- Local business and food industry news, especially around a product launch
- Halal industry publications and portals
- Event and exhibition listings

Quality matters far more than quantity. Five links from Malaysian food industry
sites beat a hundred from random directories.

### Simple habits that compound

- Every new product gets its own page on the website. Ask us to build it.
- Contact details stay identical on every platform, forever.
- Post to GBP roughly weekly. Posts expire, so it is a rhythm not a task.
- Check Analytics monthly: which pages get traffic, which CTAs get clicked,
  where enquiries come from.

---

## What not to do

- No keyword stuffing in the business name
- No fake or incentivised reviews
- No buying backlinks
- No duplicate Business Profiles for the same location
- No changing the business name, address or phone on one platform without
  changing it on all of them
